# Opening preparation

`opening-programs.js` prepares material variants while the existing v2 cover is on. It uses the scene's lights and the composer's linear target, plus a fog-free target scene for shadow depth materials. Small representatives use the real building, station, rail, park, waterfall and sea-life factories. Stock representatives include the motorway's coloured instances and double-sided distant traffic. Geometry still builds on its existing schedule. The director's cover condition, tilt, path and duration constants are unchanged.

The ground gains its surface-texture hook asynchronously. Preparation copies its current hooks without copying the large texture metadata, and derives the eventual cache key from that hook chain. The regression test checks both future day and night keys and verifies that preparation preserves the original material and render target. Material representatives remain referenced so Three does not release programs before their eventual owners use them.

`opening-uploads.js` spreads buffer and material initialization over frames. A zero-count draw into a 1-pixel target initializes attributes and uniforms without drawing scene pixels. The real draw range, render target and shadow-update settings are restored in `finally`. Transparent meshes initialize the actual front/back derivatives returned by the split renderer. Preparation uses the real scene's light-state cache with a temporary child list, restored synchronously in `finally`. The upload scan skips hidden meshes, including the individual train parts replaced by visible batches. It also prepares the building-box bounds used by station label occlusion. Conservative height bounds and groups of 64 boxes reject irrelevant ray candidates without changing the exact box test. Once the core gate and the upload queue are complete, it stops scanning.

The watched opening collects tube routes first, then builds their geometry once against the terrain. It no longer builds sea-level tunnels just to dispose and replace them. Deep links keep their existing construction path; a terrain failure still makes the original flat fallback. Initial tube resnapping, crown ribbons and surface-rail building yield between bounded pieces. The synchronous entry points remain for controls. The rail normal and bounding-sphere routines use the existing bit-identical sliced geometry helpers. `yield-main.js` shares a loading slice across builders (16 ms under the cover, 6 ms during the tilt and descent), then waits for an animation frame. Tiny pieces continue as microtasks rather than flooding Chromium with boosted continuation tasks. Train aiming and batch rebasing share parent world transforms and inverses within each update. Local train matrices are composed again only when their position, quaternion, section scale or Master ratio changes. Independent native transform comparisons cover moving parents, paused trains, camera rebasing and four Master settings. The final network callback no longer repeats the Tideway snap: the terrain and Tideway callbacks already handle both possible arrival orders, and tube completion changes neither input.

## Measuring the opening

Build each revision into separate scratch directories and serve them on the sprint's prescribed ports. Run `scripts/measure-opening-programs.mjs` through the shared GPU lock, with three interleaved live/new pairs. Use `--cpu=4 --dpr=1` for the weak setup, and `--saved=night` for the saved-night case. Each boot uses a new headless Metal browser, disabled HTTP caching, and an attached CPU-throttle session.

The JSON contains raw RAF timestamps, public opening phases, actual WebGL `createProgram` and `compileShader` call times, GPU identity, console errors and load timings. `visiblePrograms` scores `cover-lifted` through `landed`. `gapsDescent` scores `descent-begin` through `landed`, including a gap whose preceding RAF was before the boundary. Do not substitute `gate-reveal`, which can omit the start of the descent. Check that the opening introduces no governor holds, and observe errors until ten seconds after landing.
