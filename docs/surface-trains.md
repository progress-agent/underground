## The same trains as the timetable

Every surface train is a tube train of `trains.js` (`trainSystem.allTrains`): its place is `trainStateAt(ud, simT)`, a parameter `u` along the train's curve, mapped onto the track. Nothing about a surface train is stored between frames, so:

- two loads at the same simulation time draw the same trains in the same places;
- a train advances while offscreen, and the shared pause and speed apply (the clock is `trainSystem.simTime`, which `updateTrains` advances by the frame's elapsed time times the speed, and not at all when paused);
- a train leaving a portal carries on along the surface, and one entering a tunnel carries on in its bore.

A car is drawn only where its centre is on open track that Lane R draws; in a tunnel the underground train, in its `line:` group, is the one drawn. "Drawn" follows the renderer exactly (sprint 30Sep26w integration): `buildCorridor` draws a Tube or DLR open run only when it has two or more samples, so a lone `surface` or `cutting` sample with tunnel on both sides (nine in the data: Aldgate, Victoria twice, Great Portland Street, Gloucester Road, between Sloane Square and South Kensington) is not drawn, and the network treats it as tunnel (`drawnOpenFlags`, `surface-rail.js`). Before this, lone cars stood on bare ground there for whole dwells, up to 277 m from any drawn segment (the round 2 verifier's finding).

## The mapping

### Tube lines: from the station-chord curve to the track

A Tube train's curve is a CatmullRom through the line's **stations** (the same curve as the bores). Between two stations it is a smooth chord, not the railway; on the open-air Metropolitan it cuts a bend by up to about 850 m. The track is Lane R's surface railway, which for every line covers the whole route, tunnels included (Central's main corridor alone is 4,293 samples, about 51 km).

1. **Anchors.** Each stop of the timetable (`stationUs`) finds its control point by its share of the polyline length (the formula `main.js` uses to make it), and that control point is one of the line's TfL stops within 60 m. The stop list is not one per control point where a branch carries `_stationIndices` (the District's two Fulham bridge points on the Wimbledon branch; matching by index there anchored each stop at the station before it, found and fixed during the build and held by a unit test).
2. **Track between stations.** Each station is placed on the line's track network (the nearest node within 300 m, or, since sprint 01Oct26h, an open drawn node no more than 30 m further than that: `stationNode`, `STATION_OPEN_PREFER_M`), and consecutive stations are joined by the shortest route over it (A*). The network is the line's own corridors plus every stretch of another line's corridor, Tube or Overground, its colour is laid on as a shared-track band; corridor pieces join end to nearest node within 60 m (the twin collapse leaves junction gaps up to about 32 m; since sprint 01Oct26h an open end joins an open node up to 12 m further than a nearer tunnel one, `JOIN_OPEN_PREFER_M`). A route outside 0.6 to 1.6 times the chord (plus 300 m), or wholly in tunnel, is refused. So is a route that turns back on itself, by more than 120 degrees between the stretches either side of any node (at 30, 60, 120 and 200 m): a train never reverses between two stations, so such a route is not its railway. Found in fix round 1: from Paddington the Circle's drawn tunnel has no drawn connection west towards Bayswater, so the shortest route ran east, round the junction and out along the Hammersmith & City, a Circle train on the wrong railway, folded double where it turned. Two intervals are refused this way (Paddington to Bayswater, each direction); that stretch is in tunnel, where the underground train is the one drawn.
3. **Progress.** Between two anchors a train's progress along the route is linear in `u`, so it runs at steady speed between anchors and dwells exactly at each station's place on the track.
4. **Portals.** Every change between tunnel and open track along a route adds an anchor at the chord's point nearest the portal, so the along-track part of the jump at a portal is zero by construction, **unless that would break the speed bound** (below), when the anchor is moved along the chord as little as holds it (and flagged: `portal` 2 instead of 1). 93 of the 506 Tube portal anchors are moved (every curve, each direction counted).
5. **Speed bound.** A train's surface speed is its timetable speed (12 m/s cruising) times the local ratio of track to chord between two anchors. A portal anchored at the chord's nearest point could put 770 m of track into 88 m of chord (the Circle by Paddington: 105 m/s on screen, about 380 km/h; the District by Earl's Court at 55 m/s; found by the fix-round verifier). Every stretch between anchors now holds a ratio between 0.6 and 1.6 (`SPEED_RATIO_MIN`, `SPEED_RATIO_MAX`), or the interval's own ratio between its stations where that is already outside them. On screen the fastest car, measured over 1,800 s of every train, runs at 1.69 times cruise (20.3 m/s, about 73 km/h, a Hammersmith & City interval whose track is that much longer than its chord); before the fix 99 car-steps exceeded 50 m/s and the fastest ran at 106 m/s.

6. **Ends (fix round 2; sprint 01Oct26h below).** A run (a chain of mapped intervals) that ends at a station on open track, a terminus or a station whose next interval is refused, carries on along the **drawn** track, never past it: up to 80 m (`RUN_END_EXTENSION_M`) along its end node's own piece, the way the run's own last 40 m head (`END_DIR_M`; at Woodford the last segment was a sideways junction hop, fix round 1). Where the drawn track ends sooner, the end anchor is moved back until the whole train fits on the drawn track, half the train plus 2 m (`END_CLEAR_M`) from its end (`fitEnds`), so a train dwelling there stands on the track short of the station's node, as at buffers. The end interval is compressed in proportion, from the anchor before it (a station, or a portal, which never moves: it stays where the chord passes nearest its mouth; one the speed bound moved only while the stretch beyond it, untouched, still justifies the move), widened an anchor at a time while any stretch in it would run slower than the speed bound allows. An end in tunnel is left alone (the cars past it are in the tunnel), and so is an end whose open drawn stretch is shorter than the train, or a fit that would run a stretch too slowly: there the cars past the run's end are not drawn, so the train is drawn in part, never off the track. In round 1 the extension carried on **straight** for whatever the drawn piece lacked, and the round 1 verifier found trains drawn up to 54 m past the end of the drawn track: about five at any moment, at Epping, Watford, Richmond, Stanmore, Kensington (Olympia) and, where the Chigwell interval is refused, Grange Hill. Over the 110 curves (each direction of each branch counted) 48 run ends are fitted, none skipped; the furthest is moved 68.8 m, half an S8 train plus 2 m, where the Metropolitan's drawn track ends at its station's node.

### Fairing: trains that hold together

The fix-round verifier found trains breaking apart at fixed places: adjacent cars more than 5 m apart or bent at up to 90 degrees, about four broken trains on screen at any moment, at 60 places (the worst the Central at the Woodford junction, the DLR at West India Quay and Canning Town, District and Piccadilly junctions by Acton and Earl's Court). The causes were in the runs' polylines, where Lane R's drawn pieces do not meet cleanly: a route hops sideways across a junction gap (up to about 32 m after the twin collapse), doubles back to a station node placed on a spur, or (the DLR) snaps between parallel decks every 10 m. A car took the heading of the one segment under its centre, so a car centred on a hop stood across the track.

Four changes, all deterministic (`src/surface-train-map.js` and the car placement in `src/surface-trains.js`):

1. **Spurs cut.** Where a run turns back by more than 120 degrees over 25 m either side and returns within 40 m of itself (legs up to 500 m), the out-and-back is cut to a straight step; the timetable time spent on it becomes a brief halt there. (None on the Tube with today's data, five on the DLR.)
2. **Kinks faired.** Every vertex where the run turns more than 20 degrees between the 8 m either side is a kink (real curves turn at most about 5 degrees over 8 m; the hops and zig-zags 45 to 180). Kinks within 40 m form a cluster, and each cluster's stretch, widened by the smallest margin (10 to 200 m) that achieves it, is replaced by a cubic Hermite blend between the run's own directions outside it, turning at most 3.5 degrees every 4 m (a radius of about 65 m). A junction gap is crossed the way a crossover crosses, not as a step. **The blend keeps to the drawn track (fix round 2):** at each point it may stray from the drawn track (in plan) at most 10 m (`FAIR_MAX_DEV_M`) further than the run it replaces does at the run's point nearest it, or as far as the run strays over the cluster itself (half a junction hop); the gentlest blend within that is taken, and where none keeps within it, the one straying least. In round 1 the margin widened until the blend turned gently enough; north of Canning Town that reached the run's free end, and the blend cut the DLR's curve by up to 43 m over open ground. (Paired with the run at the same arc length instead of the nearest point, a sideways hop's own length misaligned the two, and a Piccadilly junction by Acton Town was faired so sharply that neighbouring cars stood 4 m apart at 29 degrees.) Heights and the other attributes along a faired stretch are the original run's at the proportional arc length; above the ground the height above the terrain is kept (the terrain is resampled under the new plan position). Anchors inside a faired stretch are re-placed evenly in `u` between its ends, so a train crosses it at one steady speed (re-placed in proportion to the old arc length instead, a sideways hop's length came out as one fast step). 186 kink clusters are faired over all the curves (each direction of each branch counted; 72 of them on the DLR), and the DLR's map match changes piece 161 times in all.
3. **Heights.** Inside a faired stretch the train is crossing between drawn pieces, whose heights can differ by metres (two DLR decks at West India Quay, 2.6 m apart), so the height there is a cubic blend between the stretch's ends, in the quantity the grade limit uses (the lift above the ground for a Tube run, the deck's own height for the DLR), with the run's own grades outside as its slopes. Blended in proportion to the old arc length instead, one DLR car in the T4b capture pitched up 7.6 degrees between two pitched down. Within 15 m of a change of drawn piece outside such a stretch, a step in height is faired the same way into a vertical curve of about 400 m radius (`verticalFair`). Never along a drawn piece's own profile, which the cars ride as drawn: a first version that reached 30 m from every faired stretch, with margins to 200 m, put 137 DLR cars off their deck at Master 10, because the DLR's decks follow the ground, whose grades Master exaggerates.
4. **Cars coupled.** Each car stands on the rail at its centre, in its lane, and points along the chord between its two couplings, which it shares with its neighbours (the middle of each 0.6 m gap, and the train's two ends), so adjacent cars meet end to end and turn together on a curve, as coupled cars do. A coupling inside a tunnel mouth is at the tunnel's depth, so there the car keeps its centre's level instead of diving into the bore.

Measured in the app over 60 moments (every surface train, bundled routes), before and after: broken trains (a gap over 5 m or a bend over 60 degrees between neighbours) 236 to 0; adjacent pairs over 5 m apart 280 to 0; bent over 30 degrees 479 to 0. Over 40 moments the largest gap between neighbours is 2.77 m (2.45 m in round 1; 0.6 m over couplers plus a curve and a cross-slope, at the Piccadilly's junction by Acton Town) and the largest turn between neighbours 18 degrees, a DLR section on one of the DLR's real 40 to 45 m radius curves (the Tube's largest is 16.8 degrees, at a faired junction gap). Neighbours whose pitch differs by more than 6 degrees: 492 train-moments before, 155 after; every remaining Tube case checked rides the drawn rail to within 0.04 m, about a metre above the ground, so it is the drawn railway's own pitch over the coarse terrain (137 m cells), not the trains'. Kept by `tests/surface-trains.spec.js` ("trains hold together", "speed") and the unit tests beside it.

What fairing cannot remove is a junction where Lane R's drawn pieces do not meet: there the train crosses the gap as a crossover would, briefly over bare ground (the Central's Hainault loop joining the main line at Woodford, a Metropolitan junction by Harrow; captures F2 and F5). It no longer breaks apart there. The gaps themselves are the surface railway's to close; how far off the drawn track the trains run is measured below.

### DLR: snapping the profile's curve

The DLR's curve is not a chord: it is built from the DLR profile (`dlr-profile.js buildBranch`), which follows the mapped track node by node and stands on the measured decks. Each curve is sampled every 10 m and the samples are snapped onto the drawn DLR track within 35 m (its own corridors and its shared-track bands), in three dimensions, so at a flyover the train stays on the deck its curve is on. Since fix round 1 the snap is a map match over the whole curve (Viterbi), not sample by sample: each sample's candidates are its nearest point on every drawn piece within reach; a candidate costs its distance from the curve, and a step between consecutive samples costs the plan mismatch between the snapped step and the curve's own step plus 6 m for changing piece. The cheapest chain wins, so a train stays on one deck through a stretch of parallel decks and changes piece once, where its curve does. (Nearest-per-sample snapping alternated between parallel decks 5 to 10 m apart every 10 m: at West India Quay the train in the T3 capture zig-zagged between the two decks, cars floating between them; elsewhere DLR trains were drawn as crosses.) The run is then faired as above, and its 10 m anchors are held to the speed bound (0.6 to 1.6 metres of track per metre of curve) by the mean of a forward and a backward clamp, so a lump in the anchor table is spread either side of it (the DLR's fastest car now runs at 1.5 times cruise; before, three times). Two refusals, both found while testing: a snap clamped onto a segment's end is taken only within 10 m (past the end of a drawn piece every sample piled onto its end point, so near Pudding Mill Lane, where Lane R draws 160 m of at-grade track nowhere, a train halted and then leapt about 44 m), and a snap more than 8 m above or below the curve is refused (at Tower Gateway a viaduct sample landed on the Bank branch's tunnel beside it, blinking a car off). Where nothing is drawn within reach the curve point itself carries the train's progress, at least 1 m above the terrain (D-024), but is marked undrawn and no car is drawn whose centre is there (fix round 2; round 1 drew them, up to 57 m from any drawn track): 120 of 7,616 samples. Each open end of a DLR run carries on along the drawn deck it is on (up to 80 m, never past the deck's end), and a train standing at an end where the deck ends sooner is fitted onto it, its approach held at the speed bound's 0.6 rather than compressed (the DLR's anchors are every 10 m).

### Shared track

Both operators' trains run on shared track: the Tube line's trains are routed over the owner's corridor (the network includes every band its colour is on), so the Bakerloo runs on the Lioness's track to Harrow and the District on the Mildmay's to Richmond, alongside the Overground's own trains, which are untouched.

Lanes. Every train runs in the Overground's lanes, 2.6 m either side of the corridor's centreline, left-hand running. On the Overground's corridors the Tube line keeps those lanes: there the two operators really share the rails. Lane R draws track two Tube lines share as one corridor too, and there the lines are often separate pairs of a four-track railway (the Metropolitan beside the Jubilee, the District beside the Piccadilly, the DLR beside the Jubilee to Stratford): each line after the corridor's owner runs 3.4 m further out per band, easing in and out at 1 in 15 beyond the band's ends, so no car jumps sideways. ## Drawing

- In the Overground trains' style (`overground-trains.js`): instanced bodies, roofs and windows in the Overground's own materials (body and roof in its light grey, Jordan 23Sep26w; line identity stays on the track stripe).
- One InstancedMesh per profile and part, body (with its roof, which shares the body's grey, so one geometry and the body's material) and windows: six at most, and a mesh with no live cars is not drawn. No per-instance colour (D-015): variants are separate meshes.
- True proportions (D-039): each car's matrix is `trueHeadingBasis` at the rail morph's structure scale, aimed along the chord between its two couplings on the displayed track (see Fairing), so a car is its real size and shape at every Master, and it stands on the rail as the rail is morphed (the same rate-limited morph as the Overground and the surface railway).
- On a cross-slope: the rail morph pivots each vertex on the terrain under that vertex, so at a high Master the stripe follows the ground's slope across the track. A car in its lane (2.6 m off the centreline) is morphed the same way, about the terrain at its lane (`computeCrossSlopes`), so it rides the stripe. Found while testing: placed at the centreline's height, a Central line car stood 1.5 m off its stripe at Master 10.
- Junction hops: within a corridor Lane R's earthwork taper holds 4%, but a route crosses from one drawn piece to another at junctions, where a ground-level piece can meet the end of a viaduct piece 8 m up (the drawn deck simply ends). A car spanning that hop stood at up to 64 degrees (the Piccadilly by Acton Town, found while capturing). A run's track lift is grade-limited to 12%, raising the lower side, as the real railway climbs on an embankment the drawn ground piece does not show. On the DLR the limit applies to the rail's absolute height instead, the quantity the profile itself grade-limits (8%): its decks stay level over dips in the ground, so a limit on their height above the ground would lift them there (it did, at Master 10, before this was caught by the tests). Cars still follow the terrain's own slope where the stripe does (up to about 16 degrees on screen on the Metropolitan's Chiltern hillsides at Master 1.1).
- Measured after fix round 1 (01Oct26h, every surface car at three moments, a ray straight down onto the drawn rail; canonical units, 5 per real metre at Master 1): at Master 1.1 the 5th to 95th percentile is -0.05 to +0.06 units (before the fix -0.05 to +0.05), at Master 10 -0.07 to +0.24 (before -0.07 to +0.21), within half a metre real. Cars with no drawn rail under them, or more than 0.5 units below or 2 above it: at Master 1.1, 144 of 5,063 (132 before the fix), of which 95 have no drawn rail under them at all; at Master 10, 191 (192 before). The DLR's share rose at Master 1.1 from 28 to 49: cars crossing a faired change between two decks at different heights sit up to about 0.8 m below or 1.1 m above the nearer deck (West India Quay, Poplar), where before they zig-zagged between the decks. In plan, the 99th percentile distance from the line's nearest drawn centreline is 10.1 m (15.2 before); the median, 2.6 m, is the lane. The Overground's own trains (`overground-trains.js`, untouched by this sprint) are placed at the centreline's height: at Master 10 their 5th to 95th percentile is -0.76 to +0.65 units (about 1.5 m real either way), at Master 1.1 -0.1 to +0.76. Reported, not changed (the Overground must stay as it is this sprint).
- One top-level group, `surface-trains`, never `line:`: D-040's cull leaves it drawn above ground and keeps hiding the underground trains in their `line:` groups.
- Economies, always on and none of them changing a pixel: live cars written to the front of each mesh and only that range uploaded; trains more than 12 km away in plan are not written (the Overground's distance cull), nor trains whose bounding sphere is outside the camera's frustum; a train is first tested at its underground position, which is within the error bound of its surface one, so most trains cost no mapping work at all; a hidden layer or line writes nothing.
- Cost (M2 Max, indicative, other lanes on the GPU; provisional, not Jordan's M5): the per-frame update takes 0.03 to 0.11 ms at the five standard views at full CPU, and at the weak setup's 4x CPU throttle 0.09 to 0.42 ms (the street view, about 35 trains and 220 cars placed each frame). Fix round 1 added about 0.07 ms there under the throttle (0.34 to 0.41 ms; 0.26 to 0.30 ms at the river view; about 0.01 ms at full CPU): the coupling joints are one more run sample per car. The weak-machine comparison of the pre-fix head (2dac407) against the fix, one round, settles every one of the five standard views on the same Automatic rung, with frame-rate changes of -2.0 to +2.3 fps (run-to-run noise); the arrival case settles one resolution step lower (0.85 to 0.8 scale), without shadows on both sides; every view stays at 49 fps or more against the 30 fps bar. Against Lane R's head (the first round's measurement) the street view sits close to Automatic's 19 ms shadow line, so on the weak setup Automatic drops shadows there more often than on Lane R's head. Reductions made in the first round: body and roof merged (nine draws to six), a segment search that starts from the previous car's. Fix round 2 (round 1's head `dfaeb96` against the fix, weak setup, two alternating rounds): the five standard views settle on the same Automatic rung, except the river view in the second round, where the fix settled one rung better (L3 to L2); frame-rate changes are -1.9 to +1.5 fps (run-to-run noise); the arrival case settles on a better rung in both rounds (L4 to L3, L4 to L2); every view stays at 48.9 fps or more against the 30 fps bar. Building the mappings takes about 11% longer (359 ms against 323 ms over the 220 curve mappings of a load, spread over frames within the 6 ms budget), for the drawn-track distance checks in fairing. More DLR cars are drawn (whole trains at the ends of their curves: about 8% more DLR car positions over 40 moments, 7,408 to 8,037).
- Mappings are built lazily, at most 6 ms of them per frame (about 160 ms for all 110 curves on the M2 Max since fix round 1, about 75 ms before: the fairing and the DLR's map match), and rebuilt when a curve is (a resnap, or the DLR at a Master change); while a curve's new mapping waits, its train keeps its last one.
