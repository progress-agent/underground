# Explorable London water, sprint 06Nov26f

The generated [inventory](water-inventory.md) records every source reservoir from the existing fifty records, the specified docks and both Hyde Park waters. Duplicate source way/relation aliases are merged. Staines and King George V each have two separate basins. The modern dock outlines come from OpenStreetMap, including the reduced Export Dock now named Eden Dock.

Run `node scripts/prepare-deep-water.mjs` to regenerate the inventory and runtime JSON from the cached Overpass answers and `data/water-facts.json`. `--refresh` refreshes the named waters. The reservoir source list is the existing `public/data/reservoirs.json`; `scripts/.cache/deep-water-inventory-overpass.json` holds their current tags, parent relations and dock outlines. The JSON records source-response SHA256 values. There are no source photos or proprietary assets.

The Environment Agency's 1 m LIDAR Composite DTM supplies missing bank crests. `node scripts/measure-water-banks.mjs` fetches bounded TIFFs into the worktree's ignored cache and records every transect, query URL and SHA256 in `data/water-bank-profiles.json`. Crest is the median of maxima within 35 m outward of the mapped waterline. The raw outward transects continue to 120 m. An outside 10th percentile identifies the low surrounding floodplain when an adjacent raised reservoir contaminates the median. These are terrain measurements, not inferred depths beneath water. Environment Agency data is used under the Open Government Licence v3.0, with attribution in the derivative.

Published full-water references take precedence over estimates: Queen Mary 22.56 m AOD, Island Barn 17.59 m, and the West India/Millwall full impound 4.23 m ODN. King George VI 29m is a UKCEH lake elevation; Staines North 26m and South 23m are EA water-body altitudes. Their sources do not identify those values as operating TWLs. Bessborough and Knight retain a historical MWB 65ft OD reference, not a contemporary water-level survey. The 2023 operator case study supplies King George V's 20.18 m TWL and 22.50 m crest, filling a gap in the research delivery. The operator now publishes a maximum 9 m depth for Royal Albert. Royal Victoria uses an explicit 9.1 m illustrative estimate within the research's historical 8.5 to 9.5 m range, rather than labelling an unavailable page as a measured survey. The inherited Royal Docks reference remains 4.26 m AOD. That original Newham PDF is now unavailable, as the research notes. Greenland/South Dock and unsourced reservoir TWLs are explicit estimates in the facts, not presented as live operating levels.

A maximum or typical sourced depth defines an illustrative level bed, with estimated 3:1 shore slopes on simple reservoir outlines and vertical sides on docks and reservoirs with mapped islands. This is a permitted unsurveyed bathymetric model. The historical Metropolitan Water Board table supplies 42 ft depths for Knight and Bessborough and separate 39 ft / 29.5 ft depths for Staines North and South. Queen Mary's 11.6 m maximum comes from the Royal Holloway thesis rather than an unsourced maximum. Where sources disagree, the facts record the choice and conflict.

The banks, basin, top and navigation all use canonical coordinates. Collisions interpolate the actual rendered triangles. Landscape and depth stretch with Master. Animals reuse the Thames' own procedural builders and inverse Master scaling, so their lengths and proportions stay fixed. The same species appear in the larger habitats, with fewer individuals than the Thames. Animals animate only in the active submerged body. Mapped islands are excluded from both the volume and the physical water predicate.

Opaque basin floors and walls draw after the existing non-depth-writing terrain skin. While submerged in a new body, the existing water-top distortion mask also removes the old terrain skin along rays through the translucent surface. A separate terrain material is swapped in only around the submerged render call and then restored. This adds no scene pass or polygon loop, and every exterior view retains the original terrain shader. It avoids carving sub-grid banks into the coarse city mesh. Bank toes are clipped away from all neighbouring mapped wet polygons, including unchanged shallow lagoons and the Staines divider. All faces are also clipped to the actual map edge. Wraysbury's water is wholly outside that edge and is therefore absent, as on live; its figures remain in the inventory for traceability. The map is not extended to include it.

The original 2D builder is retained exactly for unknown or shallow water. Low Maynard and Walthamstow No. 2 are shallow, the Serpentine's typical depth is only about 2.4 m, and the Long Water has no reliable separate depth. West Reservoir has only an old combined capacity with East Reservoir. All retain live artwork, surfaces and navigation. No animal is added to a 2D body.

The deep-water module, JSON and geometry start after landing, under the existing late-layer scheduler. They never join the opening's readiness gate. The shared `isSubmergedAt` in `main.js` selects the new body or the original Thames/dock fallback. Readout, labels, atmosphere, sound and swimming use that same result. The Thames itself and its models, bed, timetable and controls retain their default path.

Feet convert at 0.3048 m/ft, rounded to two decimal places for these published levels. The selected Lockwood target is a sourced target, not a claim about today's operating state. Corrected water levels move the illustrative bed by the same amount, preserving each sourced depth; freeboard and bank geometry are rebuilt from the corrected crest and water. Original EA crest provenance remains under `bank.terrainMeasurement` where replaced. The five published water references resolve the assumed-freeboard input, while the historical/current-operation caveats above remain material to W2.

**W2 remains unmet for four reservoirs with no stated sourced TWL.** Banbury retains its 14.576 m estimate (EA crest 16.076 minus assumed 1.5 m freeboard). King George VI retains 29 m, Staines North 26 m and Staines South 23 m; their rounded lake elevations/altitudes are explicitly **estimates of operating water** in both JSON and the inventory. The [Watts, Kilby and Charles 2004 paper](https://britishdams.org/2004conf/papers/watts.pdf) only offers graph-read full-condition levels (KGVI about 29.2, South about 22.5) and a derived North level (South plus about 3 m). These are not stated TWLs and do not replace the retained estimates. The research's calibrated graph estimates (29.6/22.9/25.9) remain derived estimates too.

The same Watts paper suggests KGVI's crest is about 31.1 m on its graph, consistent with the retained 31.126 m EA measurement. Staines South's graph-read crest is about 24.4 m and its derived crest 25.0 m, both within 0.5 m of the retained 24.545 m EA measurement. No numerical crest was found for Banbury or Staines North. Each comparison and citation is recorded in the bank data. The [EA National LIDAR Programme documentation](https://www.data.gov.uk/dataset/f0db0249-f17b-4036-9e65-309148c97ce4/national-lidar-programme) says water-body no-data areas are filled, so wet raster pixels are not used as an independent water-level survey.

Each body's clipped geometry supplies a conservative render box. Whole off-screen bodies are culled before traversal, and inactive animal groups are detached from the scene graph while retaining their models and navigation APIs. Entering that body's water reattaches its animals. These landscape meshes cast no shadows, so their camera culling does not remove off-screen shadow casters.

The UKCEH portal supplies the typical depths for [William Girling](https://uklakes.ceh.ac.uk/detail.html?wbid=41659) (12.2m), [Banbury](https://uklakes.ceh.ac.uk/detail.html?wbid=47003) (7.7m) and [Lockwood](https://uklakes.ceh.ac.uk/detail.html?wbid=41865) (6.3m), in place of the research delivery's capacity/area derivations. Each has a separate `typicalSource`; the earlier derived mean remains as `historicCapacityMeanM`. The maximum depths and all rendered geometry stay the same.
